## 3. My Editorial Philosophy

Good writing must do more than sound convincing.

A piece of writing should communicate its message clearly, present information responsibly, maintain logical consistency, and satisfy the purpose for which it was written.

A polished sentence can still contain a factual error. A detailed article can omit an essential qualification. A persuasive response can introduce claims that its evidence does not support.

For these reasons, I separate writing quality from factual reliability.

My editorial approach considers five fundamental questions:

1. **Accuracy:** Are the claims supported by reliable evidence?
2. **Clarity:** Can the intended audience understand the message?
3. **Coherence:** Do the ideas connect logically without contradictions?
4. **Relevance:** Does the content address the actual assignment or question?
5. **Integrity:** Does the writing avoid invented details, misleading wording, and unsupported conclusions?

These questions guide both my editing work and my approach to evaluating AI-generated content.

## 4. My Writing and Content Evaluation Workflow

My proposed workflow for reviewing journalistic content or evaluating AI-generated writing consists of seven stages.

### Stage 1: Understand the Assignment

Before reviewing a response, I identify:

- The central question or reporting objective.
- The intended audience.
- Required facts and essential context.
- The expected tone and format.
- Any word limits or explicit restrictions.
- The source material that must guide the response.

A response cannot be evaluated fairly without understanding what it was expected to accomplish.

### Stage 2: Read the Content for Meaning

I read the complete response before making detailed judgments.

This allows me to understand its overall argument, identify its main claims, and determine whether the content addresses the intended purpose.

I then examine individual statements that may contain ambiguity, unsupported information, or inconsistencies.

### Stage 3: Examine Factual Claims

I identify claims that can be checked and distinguish them from opinion, interpretation, prediction, and general commentary.

Where the source material is supplied, I compare claims directly against it.

For live fact-checking assignments, I would use appropriate original documents, authoritative sources, and reliable independent reporting, following the project's verification requirements.

I distinguish between a claim that has been disproved and one that simply lacks sufficient supporting evidence.

### Stage 4: Evaluate Language and Tone

I examine grammar, sentence structure, readability, tone, repetition, and word choice.

I also consider whether the writing suits the intended audience and whether its language introduces an unintended bias or creates a misleading impression.

### Stage 5: Check Logic and Internal Consistency

I examine whether the conclusions follow from the available information.

I look for contradictions, missing context, unsupported causal claims, inconsistent figures, and statements that conflict with other parts of the response.

### Stage 6: Document Findings

I record the relevant issue, its location, its significance, and the reason it matters.

My feedback should be specific enough for another reviewer to understand the decision and for a writer to know what needs correction.

### Stage 7: Make a Defensible Recommendation

I summarize the principal strengths and weaknesses and identify the most important corrections.

For AI evaluation, I compare competing responses against the same instructions and evaluation criteria, rather than rewarding a response simply because it is longer or sounds more confident.

## 5. Practical Demonstration: Evaluating AI-Generated News Writing

### Project Objective

This demonstration evaluates two hypothetical AI-generated news briefs against a supplied source note.

The exercise illustrates instruction-following, factual fidelity, neutral reporting, clarity, and evidence-based evaluation.

The library and announcement in this example are fictional. The example is designed to demonstrate an evaluation method, not to report a real news event.

### Assignment Prompt

Write a neutral news brief of three to four sentences using only the facts in the source note below.

Do not invent quotations, figures, names, or additional claims.

### Supplied Source Note

The Riverside Community Library announced on Monday, May 11, 2026, that its weekday closing time would change from 5 p.m. to 7 p.m., effective June 1, 2026.

Weekend hours would remain unchanged.

The library director said the change was intended to give students more time to study for examinations.

This source note is the controlling source for the exercise. Any additional factual claims must not be presented as established information.

---

### Response A

The Riverside Community Library will extend its weekday hours beginning June 1, 2026, the library announced Monday, May 11. The library will now close at 7 p.m. on weekdays rather than 5 p.m. Weekend hours will remain unchanged. The library director said the change is intended to give students more time to study for examinations.

### Response B

The Riverside Community Library will remain open until 9 p.m. every day starting June 1, following a survey that found 78% of local students struggle to find study space. Director Maria Evans told reporters that the change would reduce examination failure rates by 30%. The library will also introduce Sunday tutoring and free laptops for students.

## 6. Applying the Evaluation Rubric

For this demonstration, I use a five-point scale for each assessment dimension.

**5 — Excellent:** Fully meets the requirement.  
**4 — Strong:** Meets the requirement with a minor weakness.  
**3 — Adequate:** Partially meets the requirement but needs improvement.  
**2 — Weak:** Contains significant problems affecting quality.  
**1 — Poor:** Fails the requirement or contains serious errors.

The five dimensions are instruction adherence, factual fidelity, clarity, neutral tone, and overall coherence and usefulness.

| Evaluation Dimension | Response A | Response B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Instruction adherence | 5/5 | 1/5 |
| Factual fidelity | 5/5 | 1/5 |
| Clarity and readability | 5/5 | 4/5 |
| Neutral reporting tone | 5/5 | 2/5 |
| Coherence and usefulness | 5/5 | 1/5 |
| **Average score** | **5.0/5** | **1.8/5** |

These scores are illustrative judgments based on the rubric and supplied source note.

### Assessment of Response A

**Instruction adherence: 5/5**

Response A contains four sentences, uses a neutral news style, and addresses the requested announcement.

**Factual fidelity: 5/5**

The dates, weekday closing times, unchanged weekend hours, and stated purpose match the supplied source note. The response does not introduce additional factual claims.

**Clarity and readability: 5/5**

The brief communicates the essential information in direct language. The sequence of announcement, operational change, weekend clarification, and stated purpose is easy to follow.

**Neutral reporting tone: 5/5**

The response avoids sensational language and presents the information without unnecessary persuasion.

**Coherence and usefulness: 5/5**

The reader can understand what is changing, when it takes effect, which hours remain unchanged, and why the change was announced.

**Overall decision:** Response A is the stronger submission. It follows the instructions, maintains source fidelity, and communicates the announcement clearly.

### Assessment of Response B

**Instruction adherence: 1/5**

Response B introduces several claims that are not permitted by the assignment. It also changes the weekday closing time into an all-day, every-day closing time of 9 p.m.

**Factual fidelity: 1/5**

The supplied source note does not support the alleged survey result, the named director, the predicted reduction in examination failure rates, Sunday tutoring, or free laptops.

These are not minor stylistic problems. They introduce new information that could mislead a reader.

**Clarity and readability: 4/5**

The sentences are readable and the structure is understandable. However, readability cannot compensate for serious factual inaccuracies.

**Neutral reporting tone: 2/5**

The response presents invented details with the confidence of established reporting. That presentation makes unsupported information appear authoritative.

**Coherence and usefulness: 1/5**

Although the response is easy to read, its central information is unreliable. A reader could leave with an incorrect understanding of the library's plans.

**Overall decision:** Response B requires substantial revision before publication or use. The unsupported details should be removed, and the operating hours must be corrected to match the supplied source note.

## 7. Example of an Evidence-Based Evaluation Rationale

**Decision: Prefer Response A. Response B fails the assignment's source-fidelity requirements.**

Response A accurately reflects the supplied announcement, uses a neutral tone, and communicates the change in weekday closing hours without introducing unsupported details.

Response B is readable, but it changes the stated closing time and adds an unprovided survey statistic, a director's name, a projected reduction in examination failure rates, and new services. None of these claims is supported by the source note. Their inclusion creates a misleading account of the announcement.

I would select Response A and reject Response B in its current form. The priority for Response B is factual correction, not stylistic polishing: remove unsupported claims, restore the correct operating hours, and recheck the revised brief against the source note.

### Why This Rationale Works

It identifies the preferred response, provides specific evidence, explains why the weaknesses matter, and gives a clear corrective recommendation.

It does not confuse fluent writing with factual reliability.

It also distinguishes between information that conflicts with the source and information that is simply unsupported by it.

## 8. A Structured Fact-Checking Method

When evaluating factual content, I use the following questions as a guide.

### A. What exactly is being claimed?

I identify the factual statement and determine whether it is central to the article or a supporting detail.

### B. What evidence should support it?

I identify the relevant source material, original document, attributable statement, or other evidence needed to assess the claim.

### C. Does the evidence support the wording?

A source may support a narrower claim without supporting the stronger version presented in a draft.

I check whether the wording exaggerates the evidence, removes an important qualification, or implies a certainty that the source does not establish.

### D. Is important context missing?

A statement may be technically correct yet misleading when essential context is omitted.

I consider whether dates, scope, conditions, attribution, or limitations are necessary for a reader to interpret the information accurately.

### E. Does the conclusion follow from the facts?

I check whether the writer makes an unsupported causal claim or presents an assumption as an established conclusion.

### F. What action is appropriate?

Depending on the evidence, the response may require correction, qualification, clearer attribution, additional verification, or removal of an unsupported claim.

I would never treat the absence of evidence in a limited source note as automatic proof that a claim is false. I would document the limitation and seek appropriate verification when the task requires it.

## 9. Evaluating Competing AI Responses

When comparing AI-generated responses, I would assess each response independently before deciding which performs better.

My comparison focuses on five key dimensions.

### Instruction Adherence

Does the response answer the actual question and follow the requested format, scope, tone, and restrictions?

### Factual Accuracy

Are factual claims supported? Are dates, figures, names, and other details represented correctly?

### Clarity and Tone

Is the response easy to understand? Does it use suitable language without unnecessary complexity, repetition, or inappropriate persuasion?

### Reasoning and Consistency

Does the reasoning make sense? Are there contradictions, missing qualifications, or conclusions that go beyond the evidence?

### Helpfulness and Fidelity

Does the response give the intended audience what it needs while remaining faithful to the instructions and available information?

I would record a specific rationale for significant scoring decisions. A response should not receive a higher overall evaluation simply because it is more detailed, persuasive, or stylistically sophisticated.

## 10. Documentation and Quality Control

Reliable content evaluation requires more than reaching a judgment. The evidence and reasoning behind that judgment must be documented clearly.

For each evaluation, I would record:

- The task or prompt being evaluated.
- The relevant evaluation criteria.
- The response being assessed.
- The score for each required dimension.
- The supporting evidence for important judgments.
- Any factual, logical, or editorial issues identified.
- The recommended correction or final decision.
- Any uncertainty or issue requiring escalation.

### Final Review Checklist

Before submitting an evaluation, I would verify:

**Instructions**
- Have all prompt requirements been considered?
- Does the response satisfy the requested format and scope?
- Are any explicit restrictions violated?

**Accuracy**
- Are factual details supported by the available evidence?
- Have unsupported claims been identified?
- Is uncertainty represented appropriately?

**Writing Quality**
- Is the content clear, concise, and readable?
- Does the tone fit the purpose and audience?
- Are there avoidable repetitions or ambiguities?

**Logic and Consistency**
- Are the arguments internally consistent?
- Do conclusions follow from the facts?
- Is essential context missing?

**Evaluation Quality**
- Does each score match the applicable criteria?
- Is the rationale supported by examples?
- Have comparable standards been applied to competing responses?
- Are unresolved issues documented?

This process supports consistent and reviewable evaluation decisions.

## 11. Handling Reviewer Disagreement

When reviewers disagree materially about an evaluation, I would identify the reason for the difference before finalizing the decision.

My proposed process is to:

1. Identify the specific score and passage responsible for the disagreement.
2. Compare both rationales against the prompt and applicable criteria.
3. Determine whether evidence was overlooked or interpreted differently.
4. Check whether the scoring definition is ambiguous or has been applied inconsistently.
5. Document the evidence supporting the proposed resolution.
6. Request clarification from the project lead when the guidelines do not resolve the issue.
7. Apply the clarified standard consistently to later evaluations.

I would not automatically average conflicting scores without investigating the underlying reasons.

The objective is to produce an evaluation that is consistent, evidence-based, and understandable to another reviewer.

## 13. Applying My Skills to AI Content Evaluation

I understand that evaluating AI-generated writing involves more than identifying grammatical mistakes.

A response may be fluent but inaccurate, persuasive but unsupported, comprehensive but irrelevant, or concise but incomplete.

My approach is to examine the response against the actual task and the applicable evaluation criteria.

I would aim to produce evaluations that:

- Identify the most important strengths and weaknesses.
- Explain why a weakness matters.
- Distinguish factual errors from unsupported statements and stylistic preferences.
- Use consistent standards across competing responses.
- Provide clear, evidence-based rationales.
- Identify corrections that would improve accuracy, usefulness, and readability.
